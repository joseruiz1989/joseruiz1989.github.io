Project Overview

This doctoral research project focused on the optimization of GaInNAs (Gallium Indium Nitride Arsenide) dilute nitride materials grown via Metal-Organic Vapor Phase Epitaxy (MOVPE), particularly using a showerhead reactor configuration.

The main challenge of this semiconductor material is the high carbon background concentration usually incorporated during the growth, which degrades the optoelectronic properties (carrier lifetime and mobility). By exploring alternative low decomposition temperature precursors, optimizing chemical input parameters, and investigating nitrogen/carbon co-incorporation dynamics, we achieved high-quality structural and optical material features.

Complete characterization loops were performed including Atomic Force Microscopy (AFM), high-resolution Photoluminescence (PL), and Electrochemical Capacitance-Voltage (ECV) profiling. The resulting layers were integrated into multi-junction solar cell prototypes, pushing the state-of-the-art efficiency forward.
