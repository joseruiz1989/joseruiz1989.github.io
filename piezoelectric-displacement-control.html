Executive Summary

"By combining custom Michelson optomechanical interferometry, real-time optical fringe decoding, analog signal conditioning, and closed-loop digital PI control, this project stabilized a low-cost piezoelectric diaphragm disc across a ±1950 nm dynamic range with 1 nm resolution, slashing tracking errors from over 11% to 0.1299%."

High-precision nanoscale positioning is a foundational requirement in modern nanotechnology instrumentation, particularly Atomic Force Microscopy (AFM), scanning probe systems, and semiconductor metrology. However, commercial piezo-actuators and industrial displacement laser sensors (e.g., Keyence systems exceeding USD $4,500) are cost-prohibitive for resource-constrained university laboratories.

Furthermore, piezoelectric ceramics exhibit severe intrinsic non-linearities—chiefly mechanical hysteresis and continuous creep/drift—which introduce substantial geometric image artifacts, positional distortion, and trajectory divergence under dynamic operation.

To resolve these challenges, this capstone engineering project engineered an accessible, high-precision optoelectronic platform consisting of a custom-built Michelson Interferometer, analog mixed-signal conditioning, and a dual-microcontroller embedded digital signal controller hierarchy. The platform was cross-calibrated against an Asylum Research MFP-3D-BIO Atomic Force Microscope at Universidad de los Andes, verifying an ultra-low metrological optical error of 0.282%.

Scientific Context & Core Non-Linear Challenges

Piezoelectric transducers elongate under electric field excitation according to the direct piezoelectric constitutive relation $\Delta l = d \cdot V$. While offering theoretically infinite resolution, uncompensated open-loop actuators suffer from three debilitating limitations:

1. Severe Mechanical Hysteresis

Actuator displacement depends on both current voltage and polarization history, resulting in divergent trajectories between voltage ascent and descent.

  • Ascending Path Error: 11.22% relative tracking deviation.
  • Descending Path Error: 6.78% relative tracking deviation.
  • Generates distorted scan frames and loss of repeatable coordinate registration.
2. Continuous Creep & Drift

Time-dependent and thermal relaxation causes the crystal matrix to drift continuously under static or repeated excitations.

  • 6-Cycle Evaluation: Drift accumulated between 34.4 nm and 48.5 nm per cycle in open-loop mode.
  • Leads to creeping baseline errors in long-exposure nanoscopic imaging scans.
3. Industrial Hardware Barrier

Industrial metrology hardware and laser vibrometers are cost-prohibitive for developing laboratories.

  • Commercial displacement laser sensors (e.g., Keyence series) cost upwards of USD $4,500+.
  • Demonstrated that repurposing precision green lasers and optical components delivers comparable sub-nanometer metrology.

System Architecture & Engineering Modules

The system synthesizes optical instrumentation, precision low-noise analog filtering, embedded real-time signal processing, and high-voltage actuation into four tightly coupled engineering domains:

1. Optomechanical Michelson Interferometer
  • Vibration-Isolated Base: Custom-built on an isolated 1/2-inch thick, $40 \times 50\text{ cm}$ magnetic steel breadboard weighted with a dense cedar wooden frame and mounted on a 10 cm vibration-absorbing dampening pad to eliminate acoustic interference.
  • Laser Source: Industrial-grade 532 nm diode-pumped solid-state (DPSS) green laser (CNI PGL-FS-532). Operates in clean $\text{TEM}_{00}$ spatial mode with low wavelength deviation ($\pm 1\text{ nm}$), critical to preventing phase-computation errors.
  • Kinematic Bases: Custom mechanical kinematic mounts equipped with fine manual gear-reduction knobs and high-strength ring ferrite magnets (repurposed from magnetron assemblies) for sub-micron optical beam alignment.
  • Target Mirror Assembly: Front-surface aluminum mirror mounted directly on the center of the piezoelectric diaphragm disc.
  • Interferometric Relation: Displacements are governed by $d = \frac{m \cdot \lambda}{2}$. Each passing fringe cycle at 532 nm corresponds to exactly 266 nm of mechanical elongation.
2. Mixed-Signal Conditioning & Optical Detection
  • Optoelectronic Sensing: High-sensitivity reflective sensor (CNY70 phototransistor) housed within a blackened anti-reflective optical baffle tube, coupled through shielded coaxial cabling to reject ambient room-light saturation and electromagnetic interference (EMI).
  • High-Impedance Buffer: Voltage follower buffer configuration prevents loading on the phototransistor collector circuit.
  • Active Low-Pass Sallen-Key Filtering: Cascaded 2nd-order active low-pass Sallen-Key topology using Texas Instruments TL084 low-noise JFET operational amplifiers.
  • Strict 5.3 Hz Cut-off Frequency: Tuned precisely to $f_c = 5.3\text{ Hz}$ to thoroughly eliminate power-line hum and high-frequency optical noise while preserving slow, low-velocity fringe transitions without introducing phase attenuation.
3. Dual-MCU Embedded Processing & High-Voltage Actuation
  • Dual-MCU Architecture:
    • Digital Signal Controller (dsPIC30F4013 @ 80 MHz): Executes time-critical DSP operations: continuous 12-bit ADC digitization, real-time dynamic fringe normalization, inverse trigonometric phase decoding, PI error computation, and 16-bit PWM generation.
    • Communications Bridge (PIC18F2550 @ 48 MHz): Full-speed USB-CDC virtual COM port stack bridging 115,200 baud UART frames between the dsPIC controller and the host workstation.
  • High-Voltage Bi-directional Driver:
    • 16-bit PWM smoothed by an active low-pass reconstruction filter.
    • Differential operational amplifier stage yielding an output range from -18.5 V to +18.5 V (nominal operating window: -17 V to +17 V with ~11.2 mV step resolution) powered from standard ±12 V DC laboratory rails.
4. Mathematical Signal Transformation & Phase Unwrapping
  • Sub-Fringe Nanometer Extraction: Extracting sub-wavelength linear displacement from the raw sinusoidal optical intensity profile requires a 3-step mathematical transformation:
  • Step 1: Dynamic Offset & Peak Extraction: $$V_{\text{offset}} = V_{\min} + \frac{V_{\max} - V_{\min}}{2}$$
  • Step 2: Voltage Normalization to $[-1, 1]$: $$V_{\text{norm}} = \frac{V_{\text{sig}} - V_{\text{offset}}}{(V_{\max} - V_{\min})/2}$$
  • Step 3: Linear Nanometer Conversion: Continuous quadrant tracking using the $\arcsin(V_{\text{norm}})$ inverse function, phase unwrapping across quadrant transitions, and laser wavelength scaling ($\frac{532\text{ nm}}{2}$) to yield a digital display resolution of 1 nm.
Host Software & Control Architecture Dashboard: NetBeans Java Swing GUI (D-PI CTRL SYSTEM) displaying real-time dual-axis optical waveforms, dsPIC30F4013 & PIC18F2550 pinouts, TL084 PWM conditioning amplifier schematic, CNY70 5.3 Hz LPF sensor circuit, closed-loop step response ($K_p = 0.0003, K_i = 0.0030, \text{SP} = 1500\text{ nm}$), and experimental hysteresis/drift elimination plots achieving $R^2 = 0.99999773$.

Closed-Loop Control Implementation & Host Software

Digital PI Controller with Anti-Windup

The closed-loop tracking error is computed at a strictly deterministic sampling rate inside the dsPIC30F4013 execution loop:

$$e(k) = r(k) - y(k)$$

The control action incorporates a proportional correction and an accumulated integral sum:

$$u(k) = K_p \cdot e(k) + K_i \sum_{j=0}^{k} e(j)$$
  • Anti-Windup Clamping: Prevents integrator windup when actuator drive voltage nears saturation rails ($\pm 17\text{ V}$).
  • Empirical Gain Scheduling: Tuned at $K_p = 0.001, K_i = 0.003$ for sub-micron increments and proportionally scaled for multi-micron travels to prevent ringing and avoid exciting the mechanical resonance of the piezo disc.
NetBeans Java Multi-Threaded GUI
  • Developed in Java (NetBeans IDE) utilizing Swing, JFreeChart, and serial communication libraries (RXTX / jSerialComm).
  • Separate high-priority worker thread for 115,200 baud USB-CDC serial parsing to guarantee zero dropped packets during fast transients.
  • Real-time dynamic gain injection ($K_p, K_i$) without restarting MCU firmware.
  • Interactive setpoint management with visual dual-axis streaming of optical fringe voltage vs. actual reconstructed nanometer position.
  • Automated experimental data logging to CSV/TXT files for post-processing in MATLAB.
